A selection of books that have shaped my thinking.

  • Antonino Ferro, Torments of the Soul: Psychoanalytic Transformations in Dreaming and Narration (Routledge, 2015). Articulated a position about the nature of therapy and the work of thinking that profoundly resonated with my sense of what we do when we do therapy (and life).
  • W. R. Bion, Experiences in Groups and Other Papers (Tavistock, 1961). One of the essential psychoanalytic texts — and one that made me laugh out loud at times, with the sheer wisdom of Bion’s thinking.
  • Marion Milner, On Not Being Able to Paint (Heinemann, 1950; Routledge edition, 2010). Wonderful and mind-expanding to read.
  • Peter Blake, Child and Adolescent Psychotherapy (IP Communications, 2008). A great guide to my clinical work.
  • Eugene Gendlin, Focusing (Bantam, 1981). How to explore experience directly — such a fresh approach when everything seems stuck in a state of impasse.
  • Erving Goffman, Frame Analysis: An Essay on the Organization of Experience (Peregrine, 1974). Context is everything; nothing more needs to be said.
  • Anthony Wilden, System and Structure: Essays in Communication and Exchange (Tavistock, 1972). Deeply influenced my sense of how an ecology of systems composes our minds and our societies.
  • Genese Grill, The World as Metaphor in Robert Musil’s The Man Without Qualities: Possibility as Reality (Camden House, 2012). Integrates literary theory with the lived experience of reading, and meshes wonderfully with all of the above from a literary rather than a psychoanalytic perspective.
